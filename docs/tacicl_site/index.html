One loop.
Two systems.

  1. Plan

    The frozen VLM reads head and wrist images, robot state, one demonstration and its own earlier receipts. It writes up to 8 rows [dx,dy,dz,rx,ry,rz,finger] and a one-line note.

  2. Guard

    System 1 executes the rows one control step at a time and watches only the gel marker field. At ≥ 6 px axial shear that is still rising, it stops and holds the arm. The tube never gets shoved deep into the gripper.

  3. Translate

    Pad asymmetry |R| − |L| becomes shift_hand_y_mm through hand_y ≈ −0.79·asym − 0.4 mm, calibrated on separate development seeds. Lateral shear gives a low-confidence x direction.

  4. Remember

    The receipt returns the stop, the shear, the estimate and every earlier jam position. When two jams point in opposite directions, the model moves between them.

The demonstration speaks touch, too.

Official episode 37 is cut where the expert's first push stalled. That block is replayed with the exact receipt System 1 would have produced from the demo's own marker shear. Its hint points the way the expert really corrected. Before the first live call, the model has already seen jam → read touch → small shift → push once.

Questions?
Answers.

    

      
Is the model fine-tuned?
No. The model is a frozen local Qwen (qwen3.8-flash-next-iq3_s) with thinking off. Everything it learns about touch comes from its context: the receipts, the jam history and the single demonstration rendered in the same receipt format.

      
Does the policy ever see where the hole is?
No. During evaluation the policy reads only cameras, robot state, finger press depth and the gel marker field. The hole pose was used only offline, as a label on separate development seeds, to fit the shear-to-millimetre line.

      
So did the VLM learn to read touch?
Not on its own. With the jam guard and raw shear numbers but no translated hint, it scored 0/10. The gain comes from the calibrated translator plus the tactile demonstration. The VLM's job is to follow the contact routine and bracket the hole between jams, which it does reliably.

      
Why can't vision do the last few millimetres?
The hidden offset is 1–4 mm, one or two pixels in a 480×270 frame. At the rim, the tube and fingers hide the hole edge. Vision-only rollouts keep pushing; the tube jams and slides out of the gripper in 26 of 30 episodes.

      
Why is x only a low-confidence direction?
In the policy runs, lateral shear matched the hidden x offset at every first jam. In the calibration probe, with pure axial pushes, it was right only about half the time. Until that gap is explained, the receipt labels x as low confidence and the prompt only allows 1 mm x probes once y has converged.

      
What would a real robot need?
Its own calibration of the translator: known-offset push trials with the arm's repeatability as the label, or labels recovered after the fact from successful insertions. It also needs compliant, slow pushing and the runtime's safety gate on every command. The structure carries over; the numbers do not.
